Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have attracted significant research interest since their introduction in 2014. While originally focused on image synthesis, their capacity to generate realistic synthetic data has drawn them into many domains of machine learning — none more consequential than cybersecurity.

This paper provides a comprehensive survey of GAN applications in cybersecurity intrusion detection, systematically reviewing over 80 methods across four application categories: data augmentation for imbalanced IDS datasets, unsupervised anomaly detection, adversarial example generation for attack simulation, and GAN-based defence mechanisms.

**Key contributions:**
- A structured taxonomy of GAN architectures used in intrusion detection
- Comparative analysis across benchmark datasets (NSL-KDD, CICIDS-2017, UNSW-NB15)
- Critical discussion of evaluation gaps and open research challenges
